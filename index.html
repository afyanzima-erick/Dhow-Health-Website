Patients struggle to access the care and medicines they need.

Kenya’s private primary care clinics are the most accessible healthcare option for millions of people. But most operate without systems, without working capital, and without data. The result is stock-outs, inconsistent care, and patients who fall through the cracks.

01

Medicines run out

Clinics buy reactively and run out of stock daily. Patients leave empty-handed or turn to informal sellers stocking low-quality alternatives.

02

Clinic owners are clinicians, not operators

They are trained to care for patients, not to manage supply chains, forecast inventory, or run a pharmacy. That gap costs them revenue and costs their patients access to medicines.

03

Nothing gets recorded

Without software, there is no data. No way to track patients between visits, anticipate who needs help, or see patterns across a community. Care remains entirely reactive.

We operate in-clinic pharmacies so clinic owners can focus on their patients.

Dhow embeds inside existing clinics and takes on the pharmacy entirely — so that clinicians can do what they are trained to do, and patients can access the medicines they need.

Software & data

Our software records every patient visit, prescription, and medicine dispensed across our network. We use AI to predict demand, flag at-risk patients, and help clinics stay ahead of their patients’ needs.

Supply & procurement

We stock essential medicines, manage reordering, and take on inventory risk. Clinics never run out of stock again.

Staffing & dispensing

We employ licensed pharmacists who dispense medicines directly to patients, ensuring professional, consistent care at every interaction.
